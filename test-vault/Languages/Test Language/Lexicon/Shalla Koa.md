# Shalla Koa

## Meaning

A shallow-water Mer farming ground used to cultivate **Koa**, edible plants grown for food.

A Shalla Koa may also be used to herd and manage schools of fish, so the term refers more broadly to a managed shallow-water food-producing area rather than only a planted field.

## Etymology

Derived from **shalla** + **koa**.

- **shalla** — close; near; close to
- **koa** — edible plants

The expression is named for the cultivation of Koa in the shallows, even though other food resources such as schools of fish may also be managed there.

## Usage

A **Shalla Koa** is a place of cultivation and food production.

It does **not** refer to:

- the Koa plants themselves;
- the Mer who tend the farm;
- shallow water in general.

**Shalla Tap Ma** is the expression for shallow water itself.

## Cultural Notes

Shalla Koa are associated with shallow-water Mer agriculture. Koa cultivation is their defining use, but some farms also incorporate the deliberate herding of schools of fish.

This reflects a Mer form of food production in which plant cultivation and the management of aquatic animals can occur within the same managed environment.
